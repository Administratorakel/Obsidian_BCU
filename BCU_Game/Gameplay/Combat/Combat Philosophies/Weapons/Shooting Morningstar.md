# Shooting Morningstar – Weapon Identity

**BCU Game – Weapon Design Documentation**

**Combat Philosophy:** [[Juggernaut]]  
**Weapon Type:** Physical / Magical  
**Combat Identity:** Battlefield Manipulation and Repositioning  
**Status:** Core identity established; normal attack combination and individual abilities pending

---

## 1. Core Weapon Identity

The Shooting Morningstar is a chained weapon specializing in **battlefield manipulation through enemy repositioning**.

Its defining mechanic is the ability to manipulate enemy positions through normal attack combinations while reserving player repositioning primarily for abilities and [[Vector Break]].

Unlike conventional weapons that emphasize knockback, stuns, or interruption, the Shooting Morningstar focuses on changing where enemies fight.

The weapon emphasizes:

- Manipulating enemy positions.
- Gathering and separating groups of enemies.
- Maintaining enemies within effective attack range.
- Combining extended attacks with unusual attack trajectories.
- Controlling enemy positions without necessarily interrupting their actions.
- Using abilities to reposition both enemies and the player.
- Changing attack coverage through the Morningstar's retractable spikes.

**Core Design Philosophy:**

The Shooting Morningstar controls where enemies fight, rather than preventing them from fighting.

---

## 2. Enemy Repositioning

Enemy repositioning is the Shooting Morningstar's defining combat characteristic.

### General Rules

Normal attacks can reposition enemies without applying conventional crowd-control effects.

- Enemies can be pulled toward a location.
- Enemies can be pushed away from a location.
- Enemies can be gathered into smaller groups.
- Enemies can be repositioned to maintain effective attack distance.
- Repositioning does not automatically apply stun, knockdown, or recoil.
- Enemy actions are not automatically interrupted by repositioning.

### Enemy Behavior

Enemies continue their normal behavior after being repositioned.

For example:

1. An enemy approaches the player.
2. A Morningstar attack pulls the enemy toward an impact point.
3. The enemy's position changes.
4. The enemy continues pursuing or attacking according to its existing behavior.

The enemy does not automatically fall over or become stunned.

This allows the Shooting Morningstar to manipulate the battlefield while keeping enemies active and threatening.

### Combat Implications

The player's goal is not necessarily to prevent enemies from reaching them.

Instead, the player can repeatedly manipulate enemy positions to create favorable attack opportunities.

This distinguishes the Morningstar from weapons whose primary crowd-control mechanics immobilize or interrupt enemies.

**Important:** Special abilities may still apply conventional crowd-control effects. The restriction applies to the baseline normal attack repositioning mechanic.

## 3. Normal Attack Combinations

### Shared Combination

**All three stances use one shared normal attack combination.**

Unlike weapons that use entirely different combinations for each stance, the Shooting Morningstar maintains the same general attack sequence.

The stances primarily change the Morningstar's physical configuration and attack coverage.

### Normal Attack Characteristics

- Attacks involve swinging, launching, or manipulating the chained ball.
- Certain attacks can strike the ground.
- Ground impacts may create repositioning effects.
- Some attacks may gather enemies.
- Other attacks may move enemies farther away.
- Later attacks can pull enemies back into effective range.
- Not every individual attack needs to reposition enemies.

The exact number of attacks and their animations remain undecided.

### Proposed Combination Behavior

A possible combination could involve:

**Gather → Strike → Push → Pull Back**

For example:

1. An initial attack gathers nearby enemies toward an impact point.
2. A subsequent attack damages enemies in the gathered area.
3. Another attack pushes enemies farther away.
4. A following attack pulls enemies back into the Morningstar's effective attack range.

This is an illustrative sequence, **not a finalized attack combination**.

The purpose is to demonstrate how repositioning can become part of continuous offensive combat.

### Ground Impact Interaction

Some normal attacks may reposition enemies around the Morningstar's ground impact location.

Other attacks may only reposition enemies directly struck by the ball or its spikes.

**Current decision:** The affected area depends on the specific attack within the combination.

The exact contact rules will be established when designing individual attacks.

## 4. Morningstar Construction

The Shooting Morningstar consists of a chained ball with retractable spikes.

The weapon's configuration changes depending on the equipped stance.

### Ball

The ball is the primary source of normal attack damage.

- Can be swung along extended trajectories.
- Can strike enemies directly.
- Can strike the ground.
- Can be launched during selected abilities.
- Can be manipulated independently through chain movement.

### Retractable Spikes

The ball's spikes can extend or retract.

Their primary purpose is to modify attack coverage and the number of enemies that can be caught.

**Current baseline:**

Normal attack damage is primarily determined by the ball itself.

Separate spike damage is not required.

Additional spike damage mechanics remain possible but are not part of the established identity.

---

## 5. Attack Stances

The Shooting Morningstar supports three stances:

- Standard
- Wide
- Focused

All stances use the same general attack combination.

The main differences concern the Morningstar's spike configuration, attack coverage, and effectiveness when manipulating enemies.

### Standard Stance – Balanced Manipulation

**Identity:** Balanced enemy repositioning.

- Spikes remain partially extended.
- Balanced attack coverage.
- Suitable for manipulating individual enemies and smaller groups.
- Provides a middle ground between Wide and Focused.

Standard Stance does not require additional unique mechanics.

### Wide Stance – Group Manipulation

**Identity:** Manipulating multiple enemies simultaneously.

- Spikes extend farther outward.
- Larger effective contact area.
- Wider attack coverage.
- More enemies can potentially be caught during attacks.
- Particularly effective when gathering or repositioning groups.

Wide Stance does not necessarily increase the range of the chain itself.

Its primary advantage comes from the increased effective coverage of the ball and its spikes.

### Focused Stance – Precise Manipulation

**Identity:** Concentrated enemy repositioning.

- Spikes retract into the ball.
- Smaller effective contact area.
- Greater precision when targeting individual enemies.
- Reduced likelihood of unintentionally catching surrounding enemies.

Focused Stance emphasizes controlling selected enemies rather than large groups.

Potential increases to repositioning strength or damage remain undecided.

### Shared Stance Rules

- All three stances use the same general attack combination.
- Stances modify how attacks contact enemies.
- Stances do not fundamentally change the purpose of individual combo attacks.
- All three stances can participate in enemy repositioning.
- Additional stance-specific effects may be considered during ability design.

---

## 6. Player Repositioning

The Shooting Morningstar can manipulate both enemy positions and player positions.

However, these functions are deliberately separated.

### Normal Attacks

Normal attacks primarily manipulate **enemy positions**.

The player generally maintains control over their own movement.

Normal attacks should not repeatedly pull the player toward enemies or move the player across the battlefield.

### Abilities

Abilities can manipulate:

- Enemy positions.
- Player positions.
- Both simultaneously.

Possible ability interactions include:

- Pulling enemies toward the player.
- Pulling enemies toward a designated location.
- Moving the player toward an enemy.
- Moving the player around an enemy.
- Repositioning enemies before executing a follow-up attack.

These are potential applications of the weapon identity.

Individual ability mechanics will be designed separately.

## 7. Existing Pull Ability Concept

The original Shooting Morningstar documentation includes a special pulling ability.

This concept remains available for future ability development.

### Proposed Ability Sequence

1. The player shoots the Morningstar overhead toward a distant ground position.
2. The ball travels with its spikes retracted.
3. The ball strikes the ground, launching nearby enemies into the air.
4. The ball rebounds upward.
5. A timed chain-whipping animation raises the ball toward enemy height.
6. The spikes extend.
7. A larger spiked magical energy sphere forms around the ball.
8. The chain retracts.
9. Affected enemies are pulled toward the player.

### Design Notes

This ability combines physical and magical damage.

It demonstrates the Morningstar's ability to manipulate both the ball and the chain independently.

The airborne effect is specific to this proposed ability and does not contradict the normal attack rule against automatic knockdown or interruption.

**Status:** Existing ability concept preserved; not finalized.

## 8. Vector Break

**Status:** Existing concept preserved; final interaction undecided.

The Shooting Morningstar's original [[Vector Break]] concept included:

- Increased damage.
- Pulling enemies.
- Repositioning the player behind an enemy.

The current weapon identity supports player repositioning through abilities and Vector Break.

However, the exact Vector Break behavior should be revisited after the normal attack combination is established.

### Design Direction

Vector Break should complement the Morningstar's battlefield manipulation identity without simply duplicating its normal attacks.

Potential interactions include:

- Gathering enemies into an advantageous position.
- Moving the player around enemies.
- Establishing favorable positioning before a normal attack combination.
- Creating opportunities for follow-up pulling or gathering abilities.

These are possibilities rather than established replacements for the original Vector Break.

---

## 9. Intended Combat Rhythm

The Shooting Morningstar should reward players who understand enemy positioning and can manipulate groups during continuous combat.

### Example Combat Sequence

1. The player approaches a group of enemies.
2. A normal attack gathers several enemies around an impact point.
3. The player continues attacking the grouped enemies.
4. A subsequent attack pushes enemies into a different position.
5. Another attack draws enemies back into the weapon's effective attack range.
6. The player uses an ability to reposition themselves or manipulate the group further.

Throughout the sequence, enemies generally continue their normal actions.

The player is manipulating the battlefield rather than continuously disabling opponents.

**Combat Philosophy:**

Maintain offensive pressure by controlling enemy positions and attack opportunities.

## 10. Distinction From Other Weapons

The Shooting Morningstar must remain mechanically distinct from other weapons that manipulate enemies.

|Weapon|Defining Mechanic|
|---|---|
|Shooting Morningstar|Enemy repositioning through normal attack combinations|
|Hammer|Impact Zones and propagating shockwaves|
|Whip|Stability tether, orbital movement, and enemy compression|
|Magic Gauntlets|Magical force manipulation and temporary force walls|
|Twin Daggers|Rapid engagement, disengagement, and Momentum|

### Shooting Morningstar vs. Whip

The Whip specializes in gathering and maintaining enemies within a controlled cluster.

The Shooting Morningstar specializes in repeatedly changing enemy positions throughout its attack combination.

The Morningstar does not require maintaining a tethered group of enemies.

### Shooting Morningstar vs. Hammer

The Hammer prepares temporary battlefield areas and exploits them through shockwaves.

The Shooting Morningstar instead manipulates the positions of enemies directly through attacks.

### Shooting Morningstar vs. Magic Gauntlets

Both weapons can manipulate enemy positions.

However, their intended methods differ.

The Morningstar relies on its chained ball, attack trajectories, and physical repositioning.

The Magic Gauntlets rely on magical forces, palm strikes, and temporary force walls.

Further distinctions will be established during Magic Gauntlet development.

## 11. Established Decisions

The following decisions form the Shooting Morningstar's current identity:

- Battlefield manipulation is the central weapon identity.
- Normal attacks primarily reposition enemies.
- Player repositioning primarily belongs to abilities and Vector Break.
- Normal attack repositioning does not automatically stun, knock down, or interrupt enemies.
- Enemies continue their normal behavior after repositioning.
- All three stances share one general normal attack combination.
- Different combo attacks may gather, push, or pull enemies.
- Certain attacks may use ground impacts as repositioning points.
- Whether an attack affects nearby enemies or direct-contact targets depends on that attack.
- The Morningstar uses retractable spikes.
- Standard Stance provides balanced manipulation.
- Wide Stance extends the spikes for greater coverage.
- Focused Stance retracts the spikes for more precise attacks.
- Damage primarily comes from the ball.
- Separate spike damage is not required.
- The original pulling ability concept remains available.
- The original Vector Break concept remains provisional.
